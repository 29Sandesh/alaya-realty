How to Evaluate an Industrial Land Investment

A rigorous framework for institutional investors navigating zoning entitlement, power substation capacity, and arterial logistics corridors in emerging Tier-2 manufacturing hubs.

By Elena Rostova, Head of Investment Strategy • Peer-Reviewed by Arjun Singh

Share Insight

mail

The calculus of industrial land acquisition has shifted fundamentally over the last decade. Driven by the expansion of organized 3PL logistics, semiconductor supply chains, and the multi-modal freight corridors connecting Western India to Central hubs like Indore, raw industrial acreage is no longer merely a speculative hold—it is a core institutional yield-generating asset.

However, the margin for error remains notoriously slim. The difference between a high-performing logistics hub delivering a 16% levered IRR and a stranded greenfield asset often hinges on nuanced technical criteria that traditional brokers routinely overlook.

01. Zoning and Entitlement Friction

The primary gatekeeper to value creation in industrial land is zoning. Investors must move beyond a superficial reading of current municipal classifications. True evaluation requires analyzing:

  • TNCP (Town & Country Planning) Master Plan Alignment: Is the land zoned as Industrial Heavy, Light Logistics, or Agricultural (requiring CLU conversion)?
  • Right of Way (RoW) Width: Modern multi-axle freight carriers demand a minimum road width of 80 to 100 feet. A parcel with less than 60 feet frontage will fail institutional leasing mandates.
  • Gram Panchayat vs. Industrial Development Corporation Jurisdiction: Parcels under state industrial bodies (e.g., MPIDC) carry streamlined building sanctions compared to unorganized rural panchayats.
"An unentitled parcel is a liability masquerading as an opportunity until the municipal right to develop is secured with clear environmental clearance."

02. The Infrastructure Matrix

Proximity to major highways (such as the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway spur or AB Road) is merely baseline. Modern fulfillment and manufacturing tenants require rigorous utility audits:
